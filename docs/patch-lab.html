Every knob on the Mega Drive's FM chip, explained with a working synth beside it. Each section has Try it buttons: they set the knob for you and play a few notes, so you hear what it does. Change anything yourself with the sliders, play the keyboard along the bottom, and save the result as a .tfi (with a .dmp and a .y12) for the cartridge's MIDI_PATCHES folder.

The synth here follows the chip closely (its envelope rates, feedback and modulation depth), so a patch made here sounds much the same on the console. It is not cycle-exact: listen on the console before you call a patch finished.

How FM makes a sound

The chip has six FM channels. Each plays one voice built from four operators, OP1 to OP4. An operator is a sine wave with its own volume envelope. A carrier (orange) is heard: it goes to the speaker. A modulator (green) is not heard; it wobbles the pitch of the operator it feeds, so fast that the wave bends into a richer shape. The louder a modulator, the brighter the sound.

That is the whole method: pick which operators feed which (the algorithm), set each one's pitch ratio (MUL), loudness (TL) and envelope, and listen.

ALG: the algorithm

0-7 · which operators feed which

The algorithm decides the wiring. Operators feeding into another are modulators; the ones at the bottom, going out, are carriers. More modulators in a chain give more complex, harsher tones; more carriers give fuller, organ-like sounds made of separate parts. Click one to load it into the synth.

TL: total level

0-127 · 0 = loudest · 0.75 dB a step

On a carrier, TL is volume. On a modulator, TL is brightness: how much it bends the carrier. Lower numbers are louder. Every 8 steps is 6 dB, roughly half as loud.

  • Carriers usually sit at 0-10. If a patch is too quiet, lower its carriers' TL (the cartridge's LOUDNESS setting does this for you when a patch loads).
  • Modulators usually sit at 20-45. Below 20 the sound gets buzzy and noisy; above 60 a modulator does almost nothing.
  • Turn a modulator's TL down (towards 0) to brighten, up to darken. This is the first knob to reach for.

MUL: the multiplier

0-15 · the operator's pitch as a multiple of the note · 0 = half

MUL sets an operator's frequency against the note played. On a carrier it moves the pitch: MUL 2 is an octave up, 3 an octave and a fifth. On a modulator it decides which overtones appear, so it shapes the character.

FB: feedback

0-7 · OP1 only · OP1 modulates itself

Feedback sends OP1's output back into its own pitch. A little (1-3) turns OP1's sine into a saw-like wave, adding harmonics without another operator. 4-5 gets rough; 6-7 is gritty and noisy, which is exactly what distorted guitars, growling basses, cymbals and snares want. Feedback only does anything through OP1, so it is heard if OP1 is a carrier (ALG 7) or if OP1 modulates something.

DT: detune

-3 to +3 · a small fixed pitch offset

DT shifts an operator's pitch by a tiny amount, more on high notes than low ones. Two carriers at the same MUL with opposite DT (say +2 and -2) beat against each other: a chorus or slight-shimmer effect, the classic way to thicken pads, strings, electric pianos and "supersaw" leads. On a modulator, DT adds a slow movement to the tone.

The envelope: AR, D1R, SL, D2R, RR

each operator has its own · drawn in its box, the dashed line is key release

An envelope shapes an operator's level over time. Carriers' envelopes are the volume you hear; modulators' envelopes are how the brightness moves (a modulator that decays faster than its carrier is the "filter closing" of a pluck or a piano).

KnobRangeWhat it does
AR0-31Attack rate: how fast it rises when a key goes down. 31 is instant, 20-26 a soft start, 10-16 a slow swell (pads, strings). 0 never starts: silence.
D1R0-31First decay rate: how fast it falls from the peak to the sustain level. 0 = no fall.
SL0-15Sustain level: where the first decay stops, 3 dB a step. 0 = stay at the peak; 15 = fall all the way to silence.
D2R0-31Second decay rate, while the key is still held. 0 = hold forever (organ); 1-5 = a slow fade like a piano string; higher = the note dies while held.
RR0-15Release rate after the key comes up. 15 = cut off; 7-9 = a short tail; 2-5 = a long ring.

Recipes: organ = AR 31, D1R 0, SL 0, D2R 0, RR 8. Pluck = AR 31, D1R 10-14, SL 15. Piano = AR 31, D1R 6, SL 4, D2R 3, RR 7. Pad = AR 12, D1R 2, SL 2, D2R 0, RR 4.

RS: rate scaling

0-3 · higher notes get faster envelopes

Real instruments decay faster on high notes. RS makes all four rates of an operator speed up as the pitch rises: 0 = the same everywhere, 3 = much faster at the top. Use 1-2 on pianos, guitars, mallets and plucks so the top end does not ring on forever.

LFO, AMS, FMS and AM: vibrato and tremolo

LFO is shared by all six channels · AMS 0-3 · FMS 0-7 · AM per operator

The chip has one low-frequency oscillator (LFO: off, or 8 speeds from 3.98 to 72.2 Hz). Each channel decides how much it listens:

  • FMS (0-7) is vibrato depth, the pitch wobbling: from ±3.4 cents up to ±80 cents. 2-3 sounds natural on leads and strings.
  • AMS (0-3) is tremolo depth (0, 1.4, 5.9, 11.8 dB), but only on operators whose AM switch is on. AM on a carrier wobbles the volume; on a modulator it wobbles the brightness (a wah-like movement).

Two things to know. The LFO speed is global: change it and every channel's vibrato changes. And a .tfi file does not store AMS, FMS, AM or pan; use .dmp (or .y12 for AM) when a patch depends on them. The Save button here writes all three.

SSG-EG: looping envelopes

off, or shapes 1-8 · per operator · for effects

SSG-EG makes an envelope repeat or flip instead of ending. It is the trick behind the chip's buzzing, sweeping and "sawtooth wave" effects. It uses the operator's D1R, SL and D2R and ignores RR while held. The shapes, as numbered in the cartridge's editor:

PAN

L, R, LR (both) or off · per channel

Which speaker the channel plays from. Not stored in .tfi or .dmp files (it is a channel setting, not part of the instrument); in POLY mode the cartridge's STEREO SPREAD setting pans each note for you.

Saving: what the files hold

The cartridge reads .tfi, .dmp and .y12 from the SD card's MIDI_PATCHES folder (and folders inside it). Save writes all three into one .zip; keep the one you want.

FormatFromStoresLeaves out
.tfiTFM Music MakerALG, FB, and per operator MUL, DT, TL, RS, AR, D1R, D2R, RR, SL, SSGAMS, FMS, AM, PAN, LFO
.dmpDefleMaskAll of TFI, plus AMS, FMS and AMPAN, LFO
.y12GensThe chip's registers, including AM, and a 15-letter nameAMS, FMS, PAN, LFO

A .tfi is 42 bytes: the algorithm, the feedback, then four blocks of ten bytes in the order OP1, OP3, OP2, OP4 (the chip's own order). Each block is MUL, DT (3 = no detune, 0-2 = -3..-1, 4-6 = +1..+3), TL, RS, AR, D1R, D2R, RR, SL, SSG (0 or 8-15).

On the console

In the cartridge's library, choose a patch, then press A on it again to open the editor. The D-pad picks a knob; A starts changing it (up/down by 1, left/right by 10, A or B done); C moves to the next channel; START saves (over the file, or as a new .tfi, .dmp or .y12 in the folder you are in). Every change is heard at once.
